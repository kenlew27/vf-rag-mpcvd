Agentic verification-first RAG for trustworthy decision support in MPCVD diamond growth

Abstract
Agentic AI systems can generate hypotheses and experiment plans for materials research, but generation outpaces the rate at which low-throughput laboratories establish ground truth. We introduce a verification-first retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture for microwave plasma chemical vapour deposition (MPCVD) single-crystal diamond growth that combines deterministic structured-data guardrails, proposition-level claim verification, and feedback-guided retry. Six stage contracts separate evidence acquisition, verification, intervention, and graded delivery. On a benchmark derived from genuine model outputs, verification reduces the blocking-violation rate — the fraction of outputs containing at least one unsupported or contradicted material proposition — from 77% to 10% (87% relative reduction), while retry recovers delivery (the fraction of outputs returned to the user) to 90%. Two domain scientists independently rate 92% of 50 end-to-end outputs as adequately grounded (groundedness ≥ 4/5; every claim traceable to cited evidence) and 86% as correct (correctness ≥ 4/5; factually accurate), with mean groundedness 4.76/5. Scoring additionally captures scientific rigor (calibrated and appropriately bounded language), which averages 4.27/5 with 89% of outputs rated ≥ 4/5. Stage-decomposed evaluation localises failures to specific pipeline components without conflating source-grounded verification with physical scientific validation.
1 Introduction
Single-crystal diamond grown by microwave plasma chemical vapour deposition (MPCVD) is a leading platform for next-generation power electronics, quantum sensors, and particle detectors. Optimising growth requires navigating a multidimensional parameter space (substrate temperature, microwave power, gas composition, chamber pressure, and nitrogen concentration) documented across heterogeneous sources: structured experimental databases containing several hundred historical reactor runs, internal technical reports, and peer-reviewed scientific literature. Missed evidence, incorrect process parameters, or unnecessary physical runs carry material cost in a domain where individual growth campaigns require days of reactor time. Agentic AI systems can synthesise these sources and generate hypotheses and experiment plans, but generation can outpace the rate at which physical laboratories establish ground truth. The immediate pre-experimental question is narrower than scientific truth: is the evidence attached to a recommendation traceable, quantitatively consistent, appropriately scoped, and sufficient to justify scarce experimental attention?
Recent AI-scientist systems automate hypothesis generation, literature synthesis, and experiment planning.1–3 Standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds generation in retrieved evidence;4 Self-RAG integrates retrieval and critique;5 and SAFE decomposes responses into atomic facts for external verification.6 Materials informatics and active learning establish the value of structured evidence for materials discovery,7–9 and recent work in this journal has demonstrated domain-knowledge-aware question-answering for materials science,10 and reasoning-capable verification for metal–organic frameworks.11 Open-domain fact-checking datasets such as FEVER and Vitamin C evaluate claims against textual evidence,12,13 but scientific decision support further requires quantitative values, bounded experimental regimes, and causal language. A final-answer-only check does not reveal whether an error arose from intent classification, source routing, database retrieval, literature retrieval, synthesis, or verification, each of which requires a different intervention. Moreover, structured experimental records admit deterministic checks, whereas literature claims require probabilistic assessment.
We operationalise verification as a layered pre-experimental process over evidence packets, atomic claims, and complete answers. A claim is supported only when every material proposition is established by cited evidence; absent, incomplete, irrelevant, or ambiguous evidence renders it unsupported; explicit disagreement renders it contradicted. An unsupported or contradicted material proposition is a blocking violation and maps deterministically to intervention, targeted retry, and, if necessary, partial delivery. This definition checks traceability, attribution, quantitative consistency, and scope against the available record. It does not certify novelty, plausibility, causality, reproducibility, experimental confirmation, or scientific truth.
Our contributions are: (1) deterministic guardrails (parameterised SQL, schema validation, allowlisted entity resolution, and dry-run verification) that constrain structured retrieval to executed database records; (2) a three-status claim verifier with deterministic allow/intervene mapping, targeted retry, and claim-level graded abstention; and (3) stage-decomposed evaluation validated by both automated benchmarks and independent scientist assessment in an operational industrial research environment, separating generator, retriever, verifier, intervention, retry, and delivery behaviour.
2 System architecture and methods
2.1 Pipeline and evidence contracts
The architecture decomposes hypothesis-oriented decision support into six stages (Fig. 1), each with an explicit input/output contract: (i) intent classification and source routing, (ii) structured-data retrieval with deterministic guardrails, (iii) literature retrieval with learned reranking, (iv) evidence-grounded synthesis, (v) proposition-level claim verification with allow/intervene decisions, and (vi) feedback-guided retry with graded delivery. The graph uses compiled state-graph routing. Three assets require domain configuration: the database schema (here, a BigQuery table of MPCVD reactor runs recording process parameters and crystal quality metrics), the document and literature corpus, and the verification action policy. All model-based stages use Anthropic Claude models: intent classification and synthesis use Claude Sonnet 4; claim verification uses Claude Haiku 4.5; dense retrieval uses Voyage 4 embeddings. Structured records establish record-level values; documents and literature support attribution and bounded statements; none establishes experimental truth. The verifier sees only a claim and its cited evidence, assessing sufficiency proposition by proposition. Whether the architecture works equally well in other domains remains to be tested.

Fig. 1 Six-stage verification-first pipeline. Each stage emits an output governed by an explicit contract; labels distinguish deterministic, model-based, and hybrid stages.
Intent classification maps a query to four task types (lookup, summarise, compare, and generate_hypothesis) and four knowledge sources (general knowledge, structured data, internal documents, and external literature). A compiled supervisor dispatches the required evidence lanes. When both structured data and literature are requested, database retrieval runs first so that concrete findings can inform the literature query.
2.2 Deterministic retrieval and provenance
The structured-data lane separates planning from execution. A constrained LLM maps natural language to a typed query specification, not raw SQL. Backend code validates column references, operators, aggregations, and grouping; resolves entity names through dynamic lexical allowlists; compiles parameterised BigQuery SQL; performs a dry run; and returns typed records. Safety rules permit SELECT-only queries on approved tables with mandatory LIMIT clauses.
Literature retrieval combines dense and BM25 sparse retrieval through reciprocal-rank fusion and learned reranking. It filters non-substantive material, expands related chunks when context is split, and returns empty context when no passage meets the relevance threshold. In combined retrieval, database evidence can constrain the literature query. Detailed models, thresholds, and the seven-stage database procedure appear in the ESI.†
Every evidence item has a typed identifier: EV-S- for structured records, EV-D- for internal documents, and EV-L-* for literature. The synthesiser cites only identifiers in a versioned evidence packet. A SHA-256 hash over canonical packet contents supports post-hoc checks that generation used the intended evidence. Run identifiers, timestamps, and hashes are set by deterministic code, outside generation.
2.3 Claim verification, intervention, and retry
The synthesiser produces inline evidence citations and abstains when evidence is insufficient. Deterministic markdown slicing extracts semantically coherent claim units; each claim and its cited evidence are submitted as an isolated parcel to the verifier. The verifier decomposes claims into material propositions and assigns supported, unsupported, or contradicted. Universal terms and causal assertions receive explicit scrutiny: one observation does not establish a universal claim, and correlation does not establish causation. Any contradicted proposition makes the claim contradicted; a claim is supported only when all propositions are supported; other cases are unsupported. Supported maps to allow, whereas unsupported and contradicted map to intervene. Schema failure triggers one recovery attempt and then isolated claim evaluation.
An intervention initiates one status-specific synthesis retry (Fig. 2). Contradicted claims must be removed or replaced with evidence-supported content; unsupported claims must be removed, qualified as unknown, or narrowed. If all claims pass after retry, the answer is delivered. Otherwise, terminal safe synthesis removes or qualifies unverified claims while preserving verified content. This is graded abstention at claim level rather than discarding the entire response. General-knowledge-only requests bypass verification because no domain evidence packet exists.

Fig. 2 Verification and retry loop. A first-pass violation triggers one targeted retry; persistent violations lead to terminal safe synthesis.
3 Evaluation methodology
We instantiate the architecture for single-crystal CVD diamond homoepitaxy using MPCVD. The evaluation environment includes several hundred historical reactor runs in BigQuery, internal technical reports, and peer-reviewed CVD diamond literature.
Six evaluation dimensions were completed. Intent classification uses 100 queries (34 easy, 33 medium, 33 hard). Supervisor routing uses 50 queries (25 single-lane, 25 multi-lane). Structured-data retrieval covers 101 queries across 96 capability types. Literature retrieval uses 50 expert queries with 249 top-5 passages plus 128 synthetic queries. Claim verification uses 100 fixtures containing 500 atomic claims, derived from genuine model outputs and synthetically modified to balance supported, unsupported, and contradicted claims with varied evidence length, order, and source. End-to-end scientist evaluation covers 50 end-to-end pipeline outputs graded independently by two domain scientists on three 5-point Likert scales: groundedness, correctness, and scientific rigor.
The scientist evaluation used a blinded grading workbench presenting each question, the full pipeline answer with all cited evidence, and three rating scales; graders scored independently without seeing each other's ratings. Full benchmark construction details are provided in the ESI.†
Wilson 95% confidence intervals are reported for proportions. Fixture intervention accuracy treats the complete fixture as the unit; atomic action accuracy treats each of 500 claims as the unit. The no-verification baseline is analytical: 77 of 100 fixed fixtures contain at least one reference blocking violation and would be delivered unchanged without intervention. This isolates the effect of verification on the fixed candidates; it does not estimate the natural prevalence of blocking violations in unconstrained end-to-end generation. To complement this analytical baseline, we also characterise the empirical violation rate in unconstrained pipeline outputs: a separate run of all 50 evaluation questions without verification produced 43 substantive answers containing 1,203 atomic claims, of which 133 (11.1%) are blocking violations (132 unsupported, 1 contradicted); 23 of 43 answers (53.5% [38.9%, 67.5%]) contain at least one blocking violation.
4 Results
4.1 Stage-decomposed performance
Table 1 and Fig. 3 summarise the primary results. Intent classification has 99.3% task recall, 90.9% task precision, 90.5% source recall, and 88.2% source precision, but only 64.0% joint exact match because both task and source sets must match. The compiled router achieves 100.0% lane-selection accuracy (50/50). Structured retrieval achieves 93.1% semantic match. Literature retrieval reaches 70.0% Hit@5 [56.2%, 80.9%] and 69.9% Precision@5 [63.9%, 75.2%]. Fixture-level intervention accuracy is 84.0% [75.6%, 89.9%].
The stage decomposition reveals that the pipeline degrades gracefully: although joint intent accuracy is the weakest single-stage metric at 64.0%, the downstream router is perfect and structured retrieval exceeds 93%, so intent misclassification rarely propagates to incorrect evidence. The primary bottleneck is literature retrieval, where 30% of expert queries return no relevant passage in the top five, directly limiting the evidence available for verification and synthesis.

The claim verifier (Stage 5) operates as follows. After synthesis, deterministic markdown slicing extracts semantically coherent claim units from the generated answer. Each claim unit, together with its inline-cited evidence items (drawn from the typed evidence packet), is submitted as an isolated parcel to Claude Haiku 4.5. The verifier decomposes each claim into its constituent material propositions and assigns one of three statuses: supported (all propositions backed by cited evidence), unsupported (evidence absent, incomplete, irrelevant, or ambiguous), or contradicted (cited evidence explicitly disagrees). Universal terms and causal assertions receive additional scrutiny — one observation cannot establish a universal claim, and correlation is not causation. A single contradicted proposition makes the whole claim contradicted; a claim is supported only when all propositions are supported. Supported claims map deterministically to allow; unsupported and contradicted claims map to intervene. The verifier does not have access to information outside the cited evidence packet: it cannot search the web, query the database, or draw on its parametric knowledge to fill evidential gaps.
4.2 Intervention and retry
Relative to the analytical baseline, the full system reduces blocking violations reaching the user from 77.0% to 10.0%, an 87.0% relative reduction (Table 2; Fig. 4). On unconstrained generation without any verification, 53.5% [38.9%, 67.5%] of substantive answers contain at least one blocking violation (Section 3); even against this lower empirical rate, verification reduces escaped violations by more than 80%. The verifier alone achieves the 77-to-10% reduction on fixed fixtures but rejects 73% of outputs, including 6 of 23 non-violating fixtures (26.1%). Targeted retry raises delivery from 27.0% to approximately 90.0% while maintaining the observed 10.0% blocking-violation rate; it converts a terminal gate into a repair mechanism.
Verification and retry are complementary: verification alone is too conservative for practical use, rejecting nearly three-quarters of all outputs, but the retry loop recovers most rejected outputs without reintroducing violations. For a diamond researcher, the system delivers usable evidence-grounded recommendations for approximately 90% of queries while catching 87% of blocking violations that would otherwise pass unchecked.

At fixture level, 10.0% [5.5%, 17.4%] contain a missed blocking violation and 6.0% [2.8%, 12.5%] receive a false intervention. At atomic-claim level, action accuracy is 78.6%, intervention precision 77.4%, recall 95.5%, and F1 85.5%. Gold-label slices expose a deliberate asymmetry (Fig. 5): contradicted claims achieve 90.9% accuracy (n = 165), unsupported claims 100.0% (n = 165), and supported claims only 45.9% (n = 170). The verifier is calibrated for safety over throughput: it catches nearly all blocking claims at the cost of over-intervening on valid ones. This trade-off is appropriate for a pre-experimental decision-support context where an undetected error carries higher cost than an unnecessary re-synthesis.

4.3 Retrieval constraints and failure localisation
The database agent achieves 94.1% SQL execution, 94.1% row match, 93.1% required-value match, and 93.1% semantic match across 101 queries. No numeric fabrication is observed: returned values originate from parameterised queries against the BigQuery reactor-run table. Approximately 7% fail semantic match; SQL failures (approximately 6%) account for most of the gap, with remaining failures arising from row selection or required-value extraction. The deterministic guardrails eliminate fabricated numerical values (the most dangerous failure mode) and constrain remaining errors to retrieving the wrong record rather than inventing one.
On 50 expert literature queries, 35 retrieve at least one relevant passage in the top five; 174 of 249 passages are relevant. The separate exact chunk-ID benchmark (n = 128) yields 63.3% Recall@5 and 57.1% nDCG@5. The difference from 70.0% expert Hit@5 reflects a stricter relevance definition: exact matching can count a semantically equivalent sibling passage as a miss. For 30% of expert queries no relevant top-five passage is recovered. Empty-context return makes this absence explicit, but verification cannot assess evidence that was never retrieved. This retrieval ceiling, not verification accuracy, is the primary barrier to end-to-end quality on complex queries, a finding independently confirmed by the scientist evaluation below.
4.4 End-to-end scientist evaluation
Two domain scientists independently evaluated 50 end-to-end pipeline outputs spanning database retrieval (20 queries), combined database-and-literature synthesis (24 queries), and open-ended research hypothesis generation (6 queries). Each output was scored on three 5-point Likert scales using a blinded grading workbench (Table 3; Fig. 6).
Table 3 End-to-end scientist evaluation (n = 50 questions, 2 independent graders, 100 total ratings). Wilson 95% confidence intervals are shown for the proportion scoring at least 4.
Groundedness is the strongest dimension: 87 of 100 ratings are a perfect 5, and 92.0% [85.0%, 95.9%] score at least 4, indicating that nearly all claims are traceable to cited evidence. Correctness averages 4.12 (SD 0.76) with 86.0% [77.9%, 91.5%] scoring at least 4; scientific rigor averages 4.27 (SD 0.85) with 89.0% [81.4%, 93.7%] scoring at least 4. Of the 100 ratings, 94 score at least 3 on every dimension; the 6 exceptions are from a single grader and cite unit errors, insufficient independent evidence, or queries that exceeded database coverage.
Performance varies by query complexity. Database-only queries (B1, n = 40 ratings) achieve the highest scores: 95.0% grounded at least 4, 90.0% correct at least 4. Combined queries (B2, n = 48 ratings) are comparable: 95.8% grounded, 91.7% correct. Research-class queries (R, n = 12 ratings) expose a clear performance gap: groundedness holds at 66.7% scoring at least 4, but correctness drops to 50.0% (mean 3.50) and scientific rigor to 50.0% (mean 3.50); both graders assign similarly low scores on these evaluative dimensions. Across all 50 questions, the mean absolute inter-rater difference on correctness is 0.76 on the 5-point scale, with 28% exact agreement and no correctness disagreement exceeding 2 points.
Scientist free-text notes identify recurring failure modes: unit ambiguity (microwave power reported in kW instead of percentage of maximum source power), insufficient citation metadata (missing authors, DOIs), conflation of related but distinct physical properties (surface flatness versus smoothness), and over-generalisation from limited evidence. These patterns align with the residual failure modes in the ESI† and the retrieval ceiling in Section 4.3: research-class queries require broader mechanistic reasoning over literature that the retrieval layer does not always surface.

5 Discussion
The results support verification surface reduction: enforce deterministic properties before asking an LLM to judge semantic support. Parameterised SQL and provenance checks constrain value origin; claim verification checks whether synthesis remains faithful. Stage decomposition makes residual errors actionable: the 64.0% joint intent score points to multi-label planning, while routing is perfect and structured retrieval failures are largely localised to SQL execution. The scientist evaluation provides convergent validity: both automated benchmarks and scientists independently identify the retrieval ceiling as the primary barrier on research-class queries.
The convergence between automated and human evaluation is itself a methodological finding. Where the pipeline scores highest, in database-grounded factual retrieval, both the deterministic benchmarks and the scientists agree. Where the pipeline is weakest, in open-ended mechanistic reasoning (R-bucket), both evaluation modes independently localise the failure to the same upstream cause: insufficient literature retrieval. This alignment suggests that stage-decomposed automated benchmarks, when well designed, can serve as a practical proxy for expert judgement on the routine queries that dominate operational use, reserving expensive human evaluation for the frontier cases where automated metrics are less reliable.
The verification-first design pattern is not specific to diamond growth. Any domain combining a structured experimental database with unstructured literature and an LLM synthesiser faces the same traceability problem: are values correctly quoted, are literature claims faithfully attributed, and are causal statements appropriately bounded? The architecture requires only a database schema, a document corpus, and a verification action policy. Adapting it to a different materials system, such as thin-film photovoltaic deposition, battery electrode synthesis, or heterogeneous catalyst preparation, would require new domain assets but no architectural changes.
5.1 Limitations
The study is bounded in several ways. Generator and verifier are model-based and may share correlated errors; both depend on evidence selected upstream. Expert sufficiency labels may be subjective; the end-to-end scientist evaluation partially addresses this with a two-rater design (mean absolute agreement 0.76/5 on correctness, 78% exact agreement on groundedness) but does not reach the panel size needed for formal inter-annotator reliability statistics such as weighted Cohen's kappa. The evaluation record does not establish absence of benchmark leakage. Evaluation is confined to MPCVD single-crystal diamond growth, so transfer to other materials systems is untested. The analytical baseline measures intervention on fixed fixtures, not the prevalence of unsupported claims in unconstrained generation.
Retrieval misses relevant evidence for 30% of expert queries. The verifier has a 6.0% fixture-level false-intervention rate and only 45.9% action accuracy on supported claims, despite retry recovering throughput. The scientist evaluation reveals a performance gap on research-class queries (mean correctness 3.50/5), where open-ended mechanistic reasoning exposes the upstream retrieval ceiling. Finally, the system checks evidential integrity, not novelty, causal validity, reproducibility, experimental utility, or scientific truth. Physical validation remains necessary.
6 Conclusions
Verification for low-throughput experimental science is a triage problem: is available evidence sufficiently traceable and consistent to justify scarce laboratory attention? The architecture combines deterministic evidence controls, proposition-level assessment, explicit intervention logic, and targeted repair. On a fixed benchmark it reduces escaped blocking violations from 77% to 10% while retry recovers delivery to approximately 90%. End-to-end scientist evaluation rates 92% of outputs as adequately grounded and 86% as correct, with research-class queries exposing the same upstream retrieval ceiling. These results suggest that the primary investment for improving output quality should target retrieval breadth rather than verification logic. The architecture is domain-agnostic by construction; extending it to other materials systems is a natural next step.

Data availability
The full pipeline source code, including the state-graph agent, structured-data retrieval with deterministic guardrails, literature retrieval with reciprocal-rank fusion, evidence-grounded synthesis, proposition-level claim verification, and feedback-guided retry, is available at https://github.com/kenlew27/vf-rag-mpcvd. The repository also includes: (i) the CVD diamond question set (50 questions across three complexity buckets); (ii) the evaluation rubric definitions for groundedness, correctness, and scientific rigor; (iii) anonymised scientist evaluation scores (100 ratings from two independent graders); (iv) scoring scripts used to compute all reported statistics, Wilson confidence intervals, and inter-rater agreement; and (v) claim-verification benchmark fixture specifications (500 atomic claims with gold labels). The blinded scientist-evaluation grading workbench is not included in the public repository due to inclusion of session data. The structured experimental database contains proprietary growth recipes, reactor configurations, and process parameters from an active industrial research programme and is not publicly available due to commercial sensitivity. The architecture is designed to be domain-portable: adapting it to a different materials system requires substituting the database schema, document corpus, and verification action policy, with no changes to pipeline code.
